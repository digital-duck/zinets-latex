Title: 
Data-Driven Typesetting of Chinese Character Decompositions with zitex

Abstract:
Early Chinese writing, including the oracle bone script, contains many pictographs, and a character can be described by its sound, its shape and its meaning. To streamline the typesetting process of a document with chinese characters of pinyin, glosses and pictures, we create zitex, an extension for XeLaTeX that generates this layout from structured character data records. A character is a short entry giving its decomposition and the position of each part in the eleven-position Zi-Matrix notation. A Python program validates the data and writes a self-contained snippet per character and view: a one-line formula, a typeset equation with pinyin and glosses, a nested-box matrix, or a tree. A further view adds a row of pictures under the meanings (an oracle bone glyph, an image or an emoji), so that each part shows its sound, its meaning and a picture in rows that line up. A short LaTeX package includes those snippets. In a handout of 59 equations built from a database, correcting one gloss meant editing one line of data, and regenerating the 59 affected figures took under a second. The code is released under the MIT licence at https://github.com/digital-duck/zinets-latex.
